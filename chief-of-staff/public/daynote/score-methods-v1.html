Sleep & Body Scores

Experimental wellness model · sleep-body-1 · 4 November 2026

These are our own estimates. They are not Polar, Google or WHOOP scores, clinical assessments or predictions of illness. 100 is the model's best range, not a guarantee of perfect sleep or health. Research supports the dimensions; our exact curves and weights have not been clinically validated.

Seven hours is a general adult starting convention, not your measured sleep need. Longer sleep is not automatically bad. The sleep interval is not necessarily time in bed. Duration and continuity overlap; they are not independent proof. Clock consistency is not circadian phase. Very short or unusual sessions need review. Missing stages or awakening details remain unknown. Short awakenings can overlap stages and are never added twice.

Body Score

Sleep 40%, HRV 25%, resting heart rate 20%, breathing 10%, skin-temperature deviation 5%. Oxygen remains visible as context with zero weight. Each physiology input needs 14 earlier distinct dates in 28 days from the same provider, device and measurement window. Unknown-device archives do not silently calibrate a new watch.

We compare with the prior median and a robust spread (1.4826 × median absolute deviation), with noise floors of 0.1 log-HRV, 2 bpm, 1 breath/min and 0.2 °C. HRV uses a logarithm. Typical values receive the best range; low HRV, elevated pulse and large changes in either direction reduce the estimate. Breathing and temperature use deviation in either direction. Wrist skin temperature is not core temperature or a fever reading.

Each physiology subscore is 100 minus 25 times a departure, bounded to 0–100. Departure is max(0, −z−0.5, z−2) for HRV; max(0, z−0.5, −z−2) for pulse; max(0, |z|−0.5) for breathing and temperature. These are experimental engineering choices.

Missing weights are renormalized and coverage is shown. Body Score needs sleep, at least two calibrated physiology inputs including HRV or pulse, and 70% available weight. Missing is unknown. Full inputs do not mean proven accuracy. Display colors use 85, 70, 55 and 40 point boundaries; these are not clinical thresholds.

What the evidence supports

  • Polar's published method averages six component scores: duration, long interruptions, continuity, actual sleep, REM and deep sleep. It discloses some optimal ranges, but not all transformations or classification algorithms. We do not reproduce its algorithm.
  • Van Dongen's sleep-restriction experiment supports attention to duration and accumulated short sleep. Experimental fragmentation research supports continuity as another dimension.
  • Phillips's timing research supports studying regularity; it does not establish our midpoint curve or causality.
  • Chinoy's PSG comparison and Miller's six-device validation show why stage estimates deserve caution. They do not validate Pixel Watch 4 specifically.
  • Slow-wave suppression research demonstrates that physiological stages matter; it does not validate wearable deep-minute quotas.
  • A small Fitbit/ECG HRV study and Oura/ECG validation support cautious attention to measurement quality and windows, not interchangeable device measurements.

Data, dates and habits

When authorized and supported by the connected device, this model uses the available daily vital streams. Availability is checked against Google's sleep documentation and vitals documentation. We retain compact dated summaries and baseline statistics, rather than new high-frequency HR samples. Existing archives, journals and historical records are preserved.

Sleep uses its Jerusalem wake date. Habits belong to the preceding waking day under the journal's existing convention. Explicit Yes and No are compared with the following sleep and morning; skipped and missing answers are excluded. Five of each answer is only a starting gate. Raw differences, counts, missingness, rough uncertainty and adjusted comparisons remain separate. Chance, serial dependence and unmeasured confounding remain. Body Score includes sleep and physiology, so correlation with its own inputs is mathematical, not an independent discovery.
