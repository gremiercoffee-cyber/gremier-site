A testable personal model

sleep-body-2 · 5 November 2026

This is not a published or clinically validated scoring algorithm. Research informs the measurements and evaluation method. Our candidate estimates require independent personal evidence and may remain unavailable. Software tests do not establish health-score accuracy.

Personal sleep reference

Your reported ideal range and workable duration stay separate from habitual sleep. Short work nights do not automatically lower your need. After enough unrestricted, rested-night observations, the app can show an observed range for comparison; it never calls that measured biological need or silently changes your reference.

Laboratory sleep-extension research distinguishes habitual duration from individual need. Our abbreviated at-home diary is not that laboratory protocol. Its observations cannot establish an exact physiological minimum.

Weekends and missing watch data

Explicit owner-authorized usual durations can fill the Friday-night and Shabbos-nap accounting, labeled assumed. The app asks for the amounts and uses the defaults if they are left alone. Explicit corrections take priority. Leaving a default writes no fabricated watch measurement or completed questionnaire. Assumed main sleep does not train or produce an inferred score. No onset/wake times are invented for manual durations.

Friday-night sleep belongs to Saturday's wake date; a Saturday-afternoon nap is prior-day sleep when viewing Sunday morning. Manual nap totals replace detected totals. Duplicate/overlapping detected naps count once. Time calculations use Asia/Jerusalem and physical instants across DST.

Recent sleep shortfall

The app compares recorded or explicitly assumed sleep over seven calendar days with both ends of your ideal range. Missing nights remain unknown; unrecorded naps limit the comparison. It lists assumed and missing dates. The range describes uncertainty in your stated reference only, not statistical or sensor-error bounds.

Extra sleep does not subtract from earlier recorded shortfalls. This is sleep accounting, not measured physiological debt or a promise of recovery. Repeated-restriction research, dose-response research and recovery-sleep experiments show why one long night cannot be assigned a universal repayment value.

What a score would mean

Sleep Score would estimate your reported sleep quality. Body Score would estimate your reported morning energy. Both use a 0–10 self-rating multiplied by ten. A future 100 would mean an estimated 10/10 rating, not perfect health, readiness, vigilance or freedom from illness. Body uses raw sleep measurements rather than including Sleep Score itself.

No hand-picked percentage weights remain. An experimental regularized regression learns associations with earlier ratings. Sleep features are duration, estimated asleep fraction, timing consistency, recent observed sleep and explicitly reported naps. Body adds same-protocol personal HRV, resting-pulse, breathing and temperature deviations. REM/deep and oxygen remain visible context. Missingness is modeled explicitly; no missing nap is silently set to zero.

The number you see today

Sleep Score is 65% length and 35% quality, added together so one can make up for the other. Length is measured against your ideal minimum, not your habit. Quality compares staying asleep, REM and deep sleep with your last 90 nights.

Body Score places your usual at 70. HRV counts for about two thirds and resting heart rate for one third. Last night counts most, then the past week. A slow slide over recent months can lower it, but a good long-term trend never props up a bad night.

The ingredients come from published sleep and HRV research, but the exact weights are judgment calls. Neither score has been checked against how you actually felt, and watches estimate sleep stages only roughly. These are not medical measures.

Evidence before a number

The candidate needs at least 60 earlier training dates and 30 later test dates with timely independent ratings. Every test date uses earlier data only. It must improve meaningfully over earlier-median and last-rating benchmarks, show reasonably consistent improvement across time blocks, have examples and test predictions for the current device/method/missingness pattern, stay within observed predictor ranges and have sufficiently narrow empirical error. If it fails, it shows Learning or unavailable.

The exact ridge strength (10), minimum counts (90 total, with ten matching-method/device training and test examples), 10% error-improvement rule, seven-observation blocks and maximum two-rating-point error radius are app safeguards—not published sufficient-sample criteria or clinically validated thresholds. Small samples, serial dependence, repeated testing, subjective-rating noise and distribution shifts can still mislead. Passing the checks permits an experimental personal estimate, not a claim of established accuracy.

The displayed interval uses the larger ranked 90th-percentile absolute test error from the overall and matching-method/device subsets. It has no guaranteed 90% coverage. Illness/travel and untimely reports are excluded from training. Current or future ratings never predict their own day. Vendor scores are not ground truth. The optional check-in is informed by the Consensus Sleep Diary; it is not that validated full questionnaire.

Measurement and validation research

  • Chinoy's wearable/PSG comparison and Miller's validation study support caution about stages and wake detection; neither validates Pixel Watch 4 specifically.
  • Sleep-regularity research supports tracking timing, not treating a clock midpoint as circadian phase.
  • Repeated HRV research supports consistent personal measurement protocols, not universal Body Score weights.
  • Prediction-model sample-size research, validation sample-size research and TRIPOD+AI explain why fitted correlations and a minimum number of days do not establish accuracy.
  • A published unified performance model predicts vigilance under studied sleep/wake schedules. We do not translate it into an invented sleep-quality or health score.

History and habit comparisons

The earlier hand-weighted prototype is retired and preserved for comparison. Raw measurements, owner archives and journals remain. Habit comparisons use explicit Yes/No answers and observed outcomes; assumed durations and retired composite marks are excluded. Five of each answer is only an exploratory gate. Predicted-score agreement with its own inputs is not an independent physiological discovery. New data stays in compact daily summaries; high-frequency HR polling remains disabled.
