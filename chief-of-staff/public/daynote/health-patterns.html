A heads-up, not a diagnosis.

Health Journal can notify you when several current measurements are unusually different from your own recent history. It cannot reliably tell whether you are about to become ill.

What triggers a flag?

At least two readings must change by two robust baseline units in these directions: lower HRV, higher resting heart rate, higher breathing rate or higher wrist skin-temperature deviation. At least one change must be breathing or temperature, since heart rate and HRV are related. Oxygen alone does not trigger this rule.

Each reading needs at least 14 earlier nights from the same source within 28 days. Baselines use medians and median absolute deviations, with minimum variability floors. This is a conservative engineering choice, not a clinically validated disease threshold. Missing values, different devices, historical readings and summaries pending recalculation do not trigger a notification.

What the evidence supports

Physiological changes sometimes precede reported illness, but they are not specific. A prospective respiratory-infection validation study reported positive predictive values of only 4–10% in its population despite a 2% daily false-positive rate. Those results do not validate this app, its sensors or its thresholds.

Wearable research highlights confounding from exertion, caffeine/alcohol and sensor noise. Apple's Vitals approach also describes multiple outlying measurements and contextual factors; this app does not reproduce Apple's algorithm.

What to do with a flag

Check how you feel and review sleep, stress, exercise, alcohol, travel and watch fit. Watch readings cannot rule out illness, and an absent alert is not reassurance if you feel unwell. These notifications do not monitor emergencies or replace professional advice.

Alerts are at most once per date, between 9 a.m. and 9 p.m. Israel time, and quiet over Shabbos. Notifications keep detailed measurements off the lock screen. Controls are in Notification settings.
