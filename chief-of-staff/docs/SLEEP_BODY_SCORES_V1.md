# Sleep Score and Body Score

Version `sleep-body-1`, 4 November 2026. These are independent, experimental wellness estimates. Research supports measuring these dimensions; it does not validate the chosen curves, weights, colors or 0–100 scale. A score of 100 means this model's best range, not perfect health. Commercial scores are comparison signals, never targets or ground truth.

## Research reviewed

- [Polar Sleep Plus Stages white paper, September 2024](https://www.polar.com/en/img/static/whitepapers/pdf/polar-sleep-plus-stages-white-paper.pdf): wrist movement plus optical beat intervals support sleep/wake and stage estimates. Its six components are duration, long interruptions, continuity, actual sleep percentage, REM percentage and deep percentage, grouped into amount, solidity and regeneration. Polar discloses averaging the six component scores on a 1–100 scale. It also describes optimal REM near 25%, deep near 17%, and duration using preferred sleep and age recommendations. The reviewed paper does not disclose all transformation curves, interruption coefficients, or the underlying stage-classification algorithm. We do not reproduce them or copy their stage quotas. Nightly Recharge adds a separate autonomic assessment; it is not synonymous with the sleep score.
- [Van Dongen et al., randomized sleep restriction, 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12683469/): repeated restricted sleep produced cumulative performance deficits. Supports substantial duration weight and retaining dated history; does not establish our numerical curve.
- [Stamatakis & Punjabi, experimental fragmentation, 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19542260/): fragmentation across sleep stages in 11 healthy volunteers affected metabolic and autonomic measures. Supports continuity as a separate dimension. Sensor-detected awake segments are not PSG arousals.
- [Tasali et al., selective slow-wave suppression, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18172212/): physiological slow-wave sleep matters. It does not imply a wrist-derived deep-minute quota is sufficiently accurate to score heavily.
- [Phillips et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28607474/): irregular timing was associated with delayed circadian timing and academic performance in students. Observational and population-specific; clock consistency is not measured circadian phase. Our midpoint-deviation component is not their Sleep Regularity Index, which requires denser sleep/wake history.
- [Chinoy et al., 2021, comparison with PSG](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33378539/): sleep/wake sensitivity was high, wake specificity lower, stages inconsistent, and disrupted nights harder to classify. Supports duration/timing over stages while retaining uncertainty in continuity too.
- [Miller et al., 2022, six-device PSG/ECG validation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36016077/): two-state sleep/wake agreement exceeded multi-stage agreement. This study did not validate Pixel Watch 4 or our score.
- [Plews et al., longitudinal HRV monitoring, 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24334285/): repeated valid observations matter. Our requirement of 14 earlier days in a 28-day window is an engineering safeguard, not that paper's clinical threshold.
- [Nocturnal Fitbit Versa 4 HRV/ECG pilot](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41268189/): preliminary support for nocturnal RMSSD, with eight participants. [Oura/ECG validation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39686012/) shows the importance of signal-quality filtering and aggregation. Neither establishes Pixel Watch 4 HRV accuracy. Google daily aggregates do not expose enough quality information to assert ECG-level accuracy.
- [AASM/SRS adult-duration consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26039963/): adults should regularly obtain at least seven hours; requirements vary. The seven-hour scoring saturation below is a general adult starting convention, not a measured individual need or sleep prescription.

Use daily HRV/RHR/respiratory/oxygen/temperature summaries rather than requesting seconds of HR for these scores. RHR is a daily vendor-derived measure, not necessarily nocturnal or a single morning reading. Sleep is a detected sleep interval, not a verified lights-out/time-in-bed interval. ## Exact first model

One current API source per metric/date takes priority over archive duplicates; within the chosen sleep source, the longest eligible non-nap processed sleep is selected once per wake date. Days without a usable session remain unknown. Different devices and unknown-device archive measurements have separate baseline identities. Daily/nocturnal measurement windows remain separate. Device names are the best currently exposed identifiers; indistinguishable devices or firmware changes remain a limitation.

Sleep Score averages available components using these nominal weights:

| Component | Weight | Curve |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Duration | 60% | `min(100, 100 × minutes asleep / 420)` |
| Continuity | 25% | Clamp `100 × (asleep fraction − 0.65) / 0.30` to 0–100 |
| Timing | 15% | Circular midpoint deviation from the prior 28 days of the same source, with at least 7 nights. No penalty inside 30 minutes; declines linearly to zero at 180 minutes. |
| REM/deep | 0% | Show estimates in minutes; do not score. |

Continuity and duration overlap mathematically; they are useful dimensions, not statistically independent evidence. Long awake segments (at least five minutes), stage totals and coverage are descriptive aggregates, with no additional penalty that would count fragmentation twice. Overlapping short awakenings are never summed onto stages. Invalid/overlapping stages are flagged; absent stage/awakening data remain null. More than seven hours is not penalized as unhealthy. Very short main sessions and unusually long intervals get review flags; impossible duration/interval combinations are excluded. Naps do not replace explicit main sleep when identified by the provider. Shift work and split sleep can require review.

Body Score weights Sleep Score 40%, log-HRV 25%, RHR 20%, respiratory rate 10%, and skin-temperature deviation 5%. Oxygen has zero weight. Each physiological component needs 14 distinct earlier observed dates in 28 days, from the same provider/device/window. Current/future values cannot train their own baseline. Baseline center is the median; scale is 1.4826 × MAD, with floors 0.1 in log-HRV, 2 bpm, 1 breath/min, and 0.2 °C. Store the center, scale, count and current deviation alongside each component.

Physiology subscore is `clamp(100 − 25 × departure, 0, 100)`. HRV departure is `max(0, −z−0.5, z−2)`; RHR departure is `max(0, z−0.5, −z−2)`; respiratory/temperature departure is `max(0, |z|−0.5)`. Typical values receive the best range; moderate higher HRV/lower RHR are not rewarded without limit. Extreme changes in either direction reduce the estimate. These thresholds are design choices, not physiological risk boundaries.

Available component weights are renormalized, while coverage is shown separately. Body Score requires sleep plus at least two calibrated physiological components, including HRV or RHR, and at least 70% nominal weight. Missing inputs and warm-up produce unknown, not a low score. Full coverage is not statistical confidence. Color bands (85/70/55/40) are display conventions, unrelated to vendor or clinical thresholds. Numbers and textual status accompany color.

## Dates, correlations, and storage

Sleep is dated by its actual wake instant in Asia/Jerusalem. The existing journal-day convention maps sleep starting before local noon to the preceding waking day. Morning/nightly-daily physiology uses the provider's calendar date, matched to that sleep's wake date. Elapsed durations use instants across DST; timing uses circular local minutes. Do not reinterpret daily calendar labels as UTC activity instants.

Habit comparisons keep independent Sleep Score, Body Score and raw HRV/RHR/breathing/oxygen/temperature outcomes separately. They compare explicit Yes/No journal days to the following sleep/morning; skips/missing are excluded. Five usable days in each group is only an exploratory gate. Show counts, missingness, raw-unit and relative effects (temperature excludes percentages), rough small-sample uncertainty and existing stratified comparisons. Repeated testing, serial dependence, measurement error and unmeasured confounding remain. Body Score contains sleep and physiology; correlation with those components cannot be presented as an independent discovery. No numerical inference is delegated to AI.

`daynote_nightly` stores one versioned document per wake date: source IDs/device/window, local journal link, UTC interval, duration/continuity/stage aggregates, five daily values, scores, baseline center/spread/count, input coverage and missingness. This compact historical sequence is also the rolling baseline state; there is no need for a separate raw-sample database. A baseline alone loses individual dated outcomes, variability and habit joins and cannot reproduce prior nights. Do not replace history with one mean.

New API sleeps are reduced in memory before D1 writes. Before a previously stored API sleep is compacted by a recent-window refresh, its original epoch payload is copied once into `daynote_legacy_sleep` and included in private export; new incoming epochs are not added to that legacy archive. Original historical epochs and owner archives are preserved for review; no irreversible cleanup is included. No new raw HR sampling occurs by default. The optional legacy HR summarizer remains available for explicit maintenance and existing imports, but is not used by these scores. General future imports should use the existing allowlisted, hash-tracked Takeout importer, not a broad arbitrary-file endpoint.

Migration 0086 adds an indexed nightly table and per-date dirty checkpoint. Actual measurement changes mark their date; unchanged syncs do not rewrite measurements. Complete successful upstream windows reconcile removed API IDs while preserving archive records; failed windows are retained. A correction refreshes its date and the following 28 baseline days. A transaction fence prevents a concurrent import or disconnect/deletion from committing stale summaries. The endpoint processes 30 queued dates per pass; old history catches up in bounded passes. No pending queue means no source-history read and no score write. Export includes derived history; explicit full Health Journal deletion clears it too.

The home endpoint reads only compact history, not the old full recovery/load model. The existing legacy model is retained as code/history compatibility; it does not set either score or appear as a requested strain score. Habit analysis consumes canonical nightly summaries, preserving raw-outcome analyses and preventing duplicate providers being counted twice. Existing vitals charts remain available.

## Budget and verification

Automatic Google sync remains once per four hours, maximum six automatic runs/day. Seven aggregate/session streams normally need about seven API requests/run (42/day), plus pagination and at most two transient retries/request; there are no high-frequency HR page requests. A nine-calendar-day overlap checks at most roughly 54 sleep/vital records per pass when there is one main sleep/day, plus exercises/naps. Stable data costs zero measurement writes. New steady-state history grows about six source rows plus one derived nightly row/day (about 2,555/year), plus exercise, multiple-session/source records and existing history.

For changed inputs, budget one source-table write, its existing index writes, one dirty-checkpoint write, and at most 29 impacted nightly updates, conditional on changed documents. Six streams × nine overlap dates × six passes = 324 candidate source records/day under the deliberately pessimistic assumption every overlapping value changes on every pass; this is not the expected daily write count. Index maintenance counts too. [Cloudflare explains billed reads/writes and index effects](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/). Daily account-wide billing includes unrelated assistant workloads and is not claimed solved by this change.

A quiet score request uses one empty dirty-queue read, one indexed nightly range (at most 30 records), and one pending check; zero writes. A current-day refresh reads roughly 57 days of six relevant source types plus affected compact records, not seconds of HR. First migration/backfill reads existing relevant history in batches and temporarily costs more. Actual remote `rows_read`/`rows_written`, backfill size and final deployment are recorded in the delivery notes. A full-day production savings measurement and prospective validation against the owner's sleep experience remain future observations.

Tests cover missingness, bounds, stage independence, overlap, device warm-up, historical correction, source precedence, midnight/DST, idempotent sync, transaction races, deletions, index use, zero-write idle requests, habit alignment, color shading and mobile size hierarchy. All synthetic fixtures are isolated from production.
