# Meeting rounds

The reply queue is not the task inventory: scheduling correspondence may be answered while the meeting obligation remains open. The periodic organizer now reads the owner’s explicit outgoing WhatsApp meeting invitations from the bridge response log, across conversations, and recognizes a finite same-purpose outreach round. Each participant gets one checkable meeting obligation under a project visible through the existing Home/project views. Invitations are not calendar appointments.

Evidence includes the actual invitation purpose and original send timestamps. Timing or the word “meet” alone is insufficient. Incoming-only announcements, casual check-ins and unrelated appointments are not candidates. Invitations awaiting answers still count. The current pass looks back seven days (up to 400 outgoing records and 100 invitation candidates); active prior rounds are supplied to place later invitations in the same initiative rather than create another project. Separate outreach waves remain separate. This depends on messages captured by the bridge; it cannot discover uncaptured conversations.

The existing project organizer endpoint also invokes this pass, and the scheduled pipeline runs it automatically. A lease, input fingerprint and 90-second attempt interval prevent concurrent/unchanged repeat classification. The normal decision provider handles one bounded classification; no research or external send occurs. Confidence must be at least 0.95, a new round requires two distinct account/contact identities, and all IDs and groups are validated before writes. Model judgment can miss an ambiguous batch; uncertainty leaves it ungrouped.

`meeting_round_sources` preserves invitation provenance independently of item/project deletion, completion and reply dismissal. Repeated invitations to the same participant in an existing round reuse their obligation, including completed obligations. Existing eligible meeting tasks can be reused, with version/assignment guards that respect manual placement. There is no reply-based completion and no invented appointment time or deadline.

Validation: `node --test tests/meeting-rounds.test.mjs` exercises grouping, isolated/irrelevant messages, late invitations, completed/deleted obligations, existing-task reuse, locked/stale assignments, malformed plans, idempotency and concurrent passes. The full suite and build also pass.

Production verification (27 October 2026): the actual provider grouped 29 explicit student invitations under “Night Seder student meetings — 27 October 2026”. All 29 original tracker items were open, had no fabricated due date, and had reply_required=0. Database provenance checks found no broken assignments. A repeat pass created/tracked zero new items. The temporary agent-authenticated trigger used for this check was removed from the final deployment.
